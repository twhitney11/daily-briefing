STORY 01

Binghamton is hosting a SUNY-wide complex systems symposium next week

Binghamton University will host the SUNY Symposium on Complex Systems Research and Education on October 14–15, bringing together faculty and students researching everything from interdisciplinary systems science to complex-system modeling. Organized alongside the international Conference on Complex Systems, its explicit goal is to establish a SUNY-wide academic network for complex systems research, encouraging cross-campus collaborations and shared educational initiatives. The symposium is open to anyone interested, not just registered conference participants.

STORY 02

SUNY and Google just announced a shared AI research platform — and Binghamton is among the first nine campuses

Google Public Sector announced today the SUNY AI Platform, a shared research environment combining Gemini Enterprise, high-performance cloud computing, storage and access to more than 200 AI models. Initially available to researchers at nine SUNY campuses, including Binghamton, the platform aims to replace the fragmented approach of individual research groups independently assembling AI infrastructure; SUNY is also providing free Google AI training credentials systemwide. Plattsburgh isn't among the initial nine campuses, but SUNY explicitly plans to expand access — and the interesting enterprise-IT angle is how the system intends to standardize governance, research infrastructure and AI accessibility across institutions with very different capabilities.

STORY 03

The FBI says attackers compromised more than 86,000 Fortinet devices — and patching alone won't necessarily fix them

The FBI and Secret Service issued a joint advisory about FortiBleed, an ongoing campaign targeting Fortinet firewalls and VPN gateways through compromised credentials and weaknesses in older password-storage mechanisms; the advisory cites 86,644 compromised devices across 194 countries. Particularly concerning is that attackers have reportedly created additional administrator accounts, removed legitimate accounts and sold access to ransomware affiliates, meaning organizations can remain compromised even after updating firmware and resetting passwords. For enterprise administrators, the takeaway is operationally important: recovery requires inspecting local administrator accounts, reviewing authentication activity and looking for persistence rather than treating this as a conventional patch-and-move-on incident.

STORY 04

A $2 billion semiconductor deal could make upstate New York essential to the AI chip supply chain

GlobalFoundries announced a five-year, $2 billion agreement with Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) to manufacture silicon interposers at its Malta, New York, semiconductor facility, with production expected to ramp up during the first half of 2028. These components connect processors and high-bandwidth memory inside advanced AI chip packages, addressing a growing manufacturing bottleneck that exists even when chipmakers can produce enough individual processors. What's fascinating is that GlobalFoundries doesn't have to compete directly with TSMC's most advanced transistor technology to become strategically important: increasingly, the ability to connect and package computing components efficiently is becoming just as consequential as fabricating them.

STORY 05

Scientists may have discovered a bizarre cloud-forming process on Mars that we haven't observed anywhere else

Researchers using ESA's Mars Express spacecraft have developed a model explaining the Arsia Mons Elongated Cloud, a spectacular water-ice formation that emerges near a Martian volcano every morning during certain seasons and can stretch approximately 1,800 kilometers across the planet. Their findings, published October 7 in Nature Geoscience, suggest the cloud may form through homogeneous nucleation: water vapor transforming directly into ice crystals without needing dust or other particles to initiate the process, something long predicted theoretically but never confirmed in a planetary atmosphere. The mechanism appears possible because Mars's thin atmosphere and its enormous volcanoes can generate extraordinarily rapid cooling and extreme supersaturation, potentially changing how researchers model atmospheric physics on other worlds, including exoplanets.
