STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY

Teaching science becomes a skill that campuses can teach each other

Binghamton University biologist Michelle Withers has received a nearly $2 million National Science Foundation grant to spread active, collaborative and project-based STEM teaching across SUNY. Fifteen campuses have agreed to host five-day faculty institutes, and the project will train local facilitators to keep the workshops going after the visiting trainers leave. The approach treats classroom improvement as an institutional responsibility: faculty will practice lessons with peers and gather evidence about whether their changes help students learn.

STORY 02 · AI · Climate research

Unrealistic training scenarios could make climate AI more useful

MIT researchers have developed a method that optimizes synthetic emissions scenarios to train climate emulators, the fast AI models used to explore possible future climates. In their tests, deliberately irregular training scenarios helped an emulator handle unfamiliar combinations of pollution changes that conventional training struggled with. The potential payoff is cheaper, more adaptable tools for evaluating regional policies, but the researchers still plan to test the technique with a scenario generated by a full Earth system model.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

A breach settlement shows why recording an attack is not enough

A four-state settlement with the Perry Johnson companies requires stronger security and data-retention practices after a 2023 breach exposed information about 13.3 million people. New York investigators found that remote access lacked multifactor authentication and that security software recorded the intruders’ activity without being configured to alert security personnel. The companies must pay at least $300,000, with another $9.7 million due if they fail to meet the agreement’s terms; the headline figure is therefore conditional. For organizations relying on outside service providers, the case highlights the importance of testing whether monitoring actually reaches someone who can respond.

STORY 04 · New York · Connectivity

Affordable broadband gets a fix for the fine print

T-Mobile has agreed to remove data caps from its New York Affordable Broadband Act plans, expand their availability wherever it offers home internet, and let search engines discover the signup page. The state attorney general found that a 50-gigabyte monthly cap had reduced eligible customers’ advertised 25 Mbps service to 650 Kbps after they reached the limit. The agreement requires 25 Mbps service for those subscribers, with temporary reductions allowed for network congestion, showing why an affordable plan’s usefulness depends on its restrictions as well as its price.

STORY 05 · Space · Deep-space communications

Voyager’s next milestone makes distance a day-long delay

NASA is preparing public activities for Voyager 1’s expected arrival at one light-day from Earth on November 18, when a radio signal will need about 24 hours to travel one way. The agency is inviting a symbolic wave of golden lights after sunset on November 17 and plans a mission discussion on November 19 about the science and engineering of keeping the probes operating. The milestone is still ahead, but it makes the mission’s operating challenge tangible: even a simple command and reply involves roughly two days of signal travel.
