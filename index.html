STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY

The next college-access barrier is a form—and someone to help finish it

SUNY announced that Rochester’s Educational Opportunity Center will offer individualized FAFSA assistance to students and families on October 27 and 30. The system also lists upcoming online workshops, including a Spanish-language session on October 27, extending help beyond people who can reach Rochester. The practical point is that available financial aid does not automatically become usable aid: completing the paperwork is another step between a college ambition and an affordable place to study.

STORY 02 · Artificial intelligence · Global health

An AI screening tool goes where the internet cannot: four kilometers underground

Google Research’s October 23 update describes Nexus AI deploying portable chest X-ray equipment and offline AI at South Africa’s Mponeng gold mine, four kilometers underground. The system flags miners with presumptive tuberculosis for confirmatory molecular testing, bringing screening closer to workers exposed to silica dust and elevated lung-disease risk. The interesting advance is the workflow: a useful model has to work where patients are, even without connectivity, and connect its findings to follow-up care. These are deployment claims reported by Google, not evidence that an AI flag alone establishes a diagnosis or that this installation has reduced mortality.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

The system watching for intruders has a blind spot of its own

Horizon3’s newly published analysis of CVE-2026-106173 shows how an unauthenticated service in FortiSIEM can allow file writes that lead to remote code execution. The researchers also report that they found no application logs explicitly identifying exploitation, although unexpected modification times on existing scripts can offer clues. For organizations relying on centralized security monitoring, the lesson reaches beyond patching: the monitoring platform itself needs restricted exposure and independent checks of its integrity. The report demonstrates a proof of concept; it does not establish that a particular organization has been attacked.

STORY 04 · Science · Space

Dragonfly rehearses a landing it must eventually manage alone

NASA says Dragonfly has completed its most comprehensive first-flight simulation yet on flight-like avionics at Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory, combining flight software with simulated camera, inertial and lidar inputs. The test rehearsed finding a safe place to land on Titan, where the rotorcraft must operate without real-time help from Earth. That makes millisecond-scale coordination between sensors and software a mission requirement, rather than a minor engineering detail. This was a ground-based computer test, not a flight on Titan; launch is planned no earlier than 2028, with further tests on the actual flight system still ahead.

STORY 05 · New York · Cannabis industry

Cannabis revenue arrives—but the records do not line up neatly

A new Dutchess County comptroller report, covered by The Current on October 23, examines $1,565,037 in local cannabis tax receipts for sales from September 2023 through November 2025, including $1,173,778 allocated to municipalities. The report identifies six instances in which unclear or late state guidance delayed municipal distributions, and says sales figures supplied by the Office of Cannabis Management differ from dispensaries’ tax-return information. The discrepancy illustrates a less visible challenge of a growing legal market: local governments need consistent records and predictable transfers to turn sales into dependable public revenue. The report attributes differences to filing errors and payment delays; a mismatch should not be read as proof of fraud.
