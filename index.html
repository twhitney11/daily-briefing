STORY 01 · Higher education · New York

SUNY’s free-application window opens today

SUNY’s application-fee waiver period runs October 19–November 2 as part of New York’s College Application Initiative for high school seniors, according to the state’s October 13 announcement. More than 700 participating high schools are pairing the initiative with counselor support and dedicated application time, addressing paperwork as well as price. That combination matters because an affordable degree is still out of reach if a student never gets through the application process; students should check the linked program guidance for their college’s eligibility and dates.

STORY 02 · Artificial intelligence · Public benefit

Anthropic proposes tying public-benefit funding to its own growth

Anthropic announced a proposed commitment of 5% of its shares to a new Public Benefit Fund on October 18, subject to stockholder approval. The company says its board could add another 2% for each 50% increase in valuation above a baseline, using a wholly owned LLC rather than a conventional charitable foundation. The interesting test is whether an expanding equity stake becomes measurable public benefit: Anthropic says governance and valuation-milestone details are still to come, so the announcement is a proposed mechanism rather than evidence of delivered outcomes.

STORY 03 · Space · Origins

A little Martian moon could hold a big piece of the solar system’s history

NASA says Japan’s Martian Moons eXploration mission is targeting launch at 3:41 p.m. Eastern on October 19, with plans to collect material from Phobos and return it to Earth in 2031. The samples could help distinguish two competing explanations for Mars’s moons: captured asteroids or debris from a giant impact on Mars. That makes this more than a collection trip—it could clarify how material moved around the young inner solar system; NASA’s contributions include an instrument to measure surface composition and a gas-driven sampler.

STORY 05 · World · Economics

The IMF’s new outlook shows why a resilient world economy can still feel fragile

The IMF’s October World Economic Outlook projects global growth of 3.1% in 2026 and 3.4% in 2027, alongside headline inflation of 4.7% and 4.1%, respectively. Its October 13 briefing highlights an uneven picture, with energy-importing economies outside the technology value chain facing particularly difficult conditions. The report helps explain why strong technology investment need not translate into broad relief for household budgets: conflict, trade friction and fiscal pressures remain risks, and these are forecasts based on information available through October 1, not guaranteed outcomes.
