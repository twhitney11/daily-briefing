STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY / North Country

Opera’s old misunderstandings get a television-age setting

SUNY Potsdam’s Crane Opera Ensemble is scheduled to close its free fall double bill with a 3 p.m. Eastern performance today, November 15, at the Performing Arts Center. The student production pairs Gilbert and Sullivan’s “Trial by Jury,” reimagined as a televised courtroom spectacle, with Menotti’s “The Telephone,” in which calls repeatedly interrupt a marriage proposal. The staging gives student singers and instrumentalists a shared production while inviting the public to see how older comic works can speak to familiar habits of distraction and public drama.

STORY 02 · AI · Safety research

A model’s written reasoning may become a less reliable window

OpenAI’s new research examines how training changes two indicators of whether AI reasoning can be monitored: solving tasks without written reasoning and controlling the wording of that reasoning. The company reports that stronger pretraining improves the first capability, while reinforcement learning and training on another model’s outputs can increase the second. The finding matters because oversight systems use reasoning traces to help detect problematic behavior, but the authors emphasize that these are imperfect indicators: greater control did not always correspond to greater success at evading a monitor. This is the developer’s research on its own models, not a demonstration that every deployed system can secretly defeat oversight.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

A malware takedown starts at the websites that deliver it

The U.S. Justice Department says an international operation removed Gootloader malware from more than 100 servers, including compromised WordPress sites, and seized U.S. domains used to deliver and control it. Authorities describe a business in which infected computers give criminals access they can sell to ransomware operators; the department attributes at least 25,000 infections worldwide to Gootloader since 2018. The action, part of Operation Endgame, illustrates why protecting ordinary websites matters beyond the sites themselves: compromised pages can become distribution infrastructure for attacks on other organizations. Victim notification and remediation accompany the disruption, rather than treating the seizure of infrastructure as the end of recovery.

STORY 04 · Space · Early universe

Two ancient quasars make a growth puzzle harder to ignore

Astronomers using Webb and Gemini North have identified a compact pair of quasars seen as they were about 900 million years after the Big Bang, Space.com reports. The objects, powered by feeding supermassive black holes, are separated by about 18,100 light-years and offer a rare paired view of the early universe. Their enormous masses raise a sharper version of an existing question: how did black holes grow so large so quickly? The discovery challenges explanations of early growth; it does not establish that the objects violate physics.

STORY 05 · World · Population and forecasting

The population “doomsday” deadline passed—and left a useful lesson

November 13 was the date a provocative 1960 paper calculated for world population to become effectively infinite, a prediction revisited this week by ABC Science. The extrapolation followed a historical pattern of accelerating growth, but population subsequently grew much more slowly than the formula implied. Demographers interviewed by ABC explain that fertility responds to changing social conditions, making a long-running trend a poor substitute for an unchanging law. The missed deadline is a timely reminder to ask what a forecast assumes about people’s behavior before treating its precise date as certainty.
