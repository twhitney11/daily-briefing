STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY

The path to mental-health care starts with supporting future practitioners

SUNY has recognized five Scholars for Mental Health, each receiving $10,000 in Voya-funded support for expenses and fees while studying psychology, social work or psychiatric nursing. The recipients include a social-work student at SUNY Plattsburgh, alongside students at Buffalo State and Stony Brook, with interests in culturally responsive care, trauma-informed practice and community support. The awards address a practical part of expanding care: helping people complete the training to provide it, though five scholarships alone cannot establish a measurable change in access.

STORY 02 · Artificial intelligence · Health research

An ultrasound AI passes a test far from its training clinics

Google Research’s October 21 account of a prospective study describes AI that estimates gestational age from standardized ultrasound sweeps performed by operators without specialist sonography training. In a primary evaluation of 385 participants in Chicago and Nairobi, the system’s average absolute error was about 4.2 days, meeting the study’s noninferiority criterion against expert-based clinical estimates; no Nairobi data was used for fine-tuning. The promising point is portability across equipment and clinical settings, potentially widening access where specialists are scarce. This was a study of pregnancy dating, not proof that the system replaces a full prenatal examination or improves birth outcomes.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

The Wi-Fi behind everyday work needs its own patch window

HPE has issued a broad security update for its AOS-8 and AOS-10 wireless networking software, with BleepingComputer reporting 72 vulnerabilities, including eight rated at maximum severity. The affected infrastructure includes controllers, gateways and access points—the equipment that keeps workplaces and campuses connected, rather than just the laptops using the network. HPE urges customers to patch and restrict management access; its advisory says it was unaware of public exploit code at release, so the severity ratings should not be mistaken for evidence of a confirmed attack.

STORY 04 · Science · Space

Roman’s next challenge is telling cosmic change from camera quirks

NASA says the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope has entered science commissioning after completing checks of the observatory and its primary instrument. The team is now calibrating light response and image quality, including tests showing that the telescope can maintain its pointing in crowded or dusty star fields. That quiet work is essential: small changes caused by the instrument could otherwise masquerade as changes in the universe, undermining the sweeping surveys Roman is being prepared to conduct.

STORY 05 · North Country · Civic life

Early voting adds days—but often means a different drive

New York’s early-voting period begins tomorrow, October 24, and runs through November 1; North Country Public Radio reports that most counties in the region have only one early-voting location. Clinton County’s site is the Government Center in Plattsburgh, while Franklin County’s is the county courthouse in Malone, making the location and its daily hours important parts of a voting plan. The state also says registration applications must be received by October 24 for the November 3 general election, so the opening of early voting coincides with a separate deadline.
