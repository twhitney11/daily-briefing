STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY · Arts

A campus AI conversation begins with the stories only people remember

SUNY Oswego announced today that memoirist Theresa Okokon will visit on November 9 as part of a Living Writers Series exploring the tension between artistic intelligence and artificial intelligence. Her memoir, “Who I Always Was,” examines memory, family stories, identity and her father’s death, bringing lived experience into a campus conversation often dominated by what software can produce. The pairing offers a useful question for students: how does a story’s meaning depend on the person who remembers and tells it? The free public talk is scheduled for 3 p.m. in the Marano Campus Center auditorium, followed by questions and a book signing; it is an upcoming event, not a report of a discussion that has already happened.

STORY 02 · Artificial intelligence · Open standards

The portable part of an AI workflow may be a plain folder

Anthropic is donating the Agent Skills standard to the Linux Foundation’s Agentic AI Foundation, moving its name, logo, GitHub organization and domain into a shared institutional home. The basic package is deliberately simple: a folder containing a SKILL.md instruction file plus any scripts and reference materials an agent needs for a task. For organizations building repeatable workflows, a shared format could make procedural knowledge easier to carry between compatible assistants instead of rewriting it for each product. The proposed roadmap includes versioned releases, a formal proposal process and security guidance, so the governance change should not be confused with a guarantee that every downloaded skill is safe or behaves identically everywhere.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

A cloud breach turns an ignored warning into a leadership question

GMO Internet Group apologized today after unauthorized access to its Onamae.com Desktop Cloud service led to leaked information and disputed transactions, according to Associated Press reporting. CEO Masatoshi Kumagai acknowledged that internal warnings about a systemic weakness had not prompted adequate action, while the company promised compensation and a replacement security system. The wider lesson is organizational: a warning only protects customers if someone owns the response and follows it through. Those remedies are commitments, not evidence that recovery is complete.

STORY 04 · Science · Food systems

A silk microneedle could reach fish too small for routine injections

MIT and USDA researchers have developed food-grade silk microneedles with a thin shellac coating that keeps them intact long enough to deliver vaccines underwater. In rainbow-trout tests, the approach delivered vaccines to fish weighing just 1–2 grams, a difficult size for conventional needle-based vaccination, while allowing the microneedles to remain attached as fish swam. The practical promise is earlier protection against disease with a delivery method that could reduce the handling and worker hazards associated with individual injections. The researchers say more development is needed before seamless farm-scale use; the study establishes a delivery approach, not a commercially proven replacement for existing vaccination systems.

STORY 05 · New York · Cannabis policy

New York’s cannabis labor law is signed—but its rewrite is still ahead

Governor Kathy Hochul signed a bill on October 28 repealing mandatory labor-peace agreements for cannabis licensees, while negotiating a follow-up deal to remove its proposed wage board and additional disclosure rules. The distinction matters: those latter provisions remain in the signed legislation, with a January 31, 2027 effective date, unless lawmakers enact the agreed amendments in the next session. Hochul argues that the board could raise costs and push consumers toward unlicensed sellers, while removing it would also remove the planned process for recommending sector-specific wages. For workers and businesses, the next legislative action—not the signature alone—will determine the final shape of this tradeoff.
