STORY 01 · SUNY · Higher education

Paid service makes community work part of the college pathway

SUNY’s fall Empire State Service Corps summit highlights a program expanded to 1,000 paid community-service internships across 58 campuses, pairing student employment with work such as tutoring and hunger relief. Participants commit at least 300 hours a year, and those completing that service can also receive an AmeriCorps education award of up to $1,400. SUNY says applications have outnumbered available campus places four to one over the program’s three-year history, suggesting that demand for paid civic experience extends well beyond the current capacity.

STORY 02 · AI · Software development

Microsoft’s coding models make specialization the selling point

Microsoft has introduced MAI Code 2 and Code 2 Flash in GitHub Copilot, aiming the first at work such as debugging and changes across multiple files and the second at frequent, smaller coding tasks. The company explicitly says the models are not yet tuned for fundamental scientific research or very long-running autonomous work, making the intended limits part of the launch story. The practical idea is to match model capacity to the job, but Microsoft’s performance and cost comparisons come from specified evaluations and include promotional pricing; they do not establish identical savings or quality for every development team.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

Java’s memory-safety promise meets the machinery underneath

CERT-EU warns that a newly patched OpenJDK vulnerability, CVE-2026-114938, can cause crashes and, under particular conditions, allow an unauthenticated attacker to execute code. The flaw affects an accelerated Base64-decoding path on certain x86-64 processors, illustrating how software designed to manage memory safely can still inherit vulnerabilities from its lower-level implementation. The advisory’s conditions matter: ARM64 systems are not affected, and attacker-controlled data must reach the vulnerable decoder. For enterprise operators, the inventory challenge extends to Java runtimes bundled inside products, whose fixes depend on their own vendors rather than a single update to the host system.

STORY 04 · Space · Earth science

Terra’s observations are ending; its record of Earth remains

NASA says Terra will stop collecting science data on December 1 after nearly 27 years in orbit, far beyond its planned six-year mission. The aging satellite has documented changes in land use, wildfire behavior, airborne dust and smoke, and ice cover, creating a record that serves researchers as well as public-safety and resource decisions. Teams plan to use its remaining fuel to lower the orbit before switching off all spacecraft systems in January 2027, while keeping its existing data free and publicly available. The transition separates two kinds of value: a durable historical archive and the continuing flow of fresh measurements that other missions must provide.

STORY 05 · New York · Infrastructure

A better road crossing can also reconnect a stream

New York’s transportation department has released draft eligibility guidelines for Environmental Bond Act funding of culvert replacement, rehabilitation and related stream restoration. The proposal links road resilience to aquatic habitat: an undersized or poorly aligned crossing can worsen flooding while also obstructing fish movement and destabilizing a stream. Public comments are due December 4 at 5 p.m.; this is a consultation on funding criteria, not an announcement that particular construction projects have been awarded money.
