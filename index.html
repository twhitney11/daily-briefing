STORY 01 · SUNY · Higher education

Adult learners lift SUNY enrollment while international graduate numbers fall

SUNY reports a fourth consecutive year of enrollment growth, with total enrollment up 1.3 percent and the number of students aged 25 and older rising 5.5 percent. Its Reconnect program now serves 8,410 adult learners, roughly 50 percent more than at its fall 2025 launch, while international graduate enrollment fell 22 percent year over year. The split matters for campus planning: stronger demand from returning adults can coexist with pressure on graduate programs, making a single systemwide growth figure an incomplete picture.

STORY 02 · AI · Measuring capability

AI benchmarks are getting stronger results—and harder questions

METR’s latest evaluation finds that frontier models usually match or outperform skilled humans on its coding and optimization tasks, yet show weaker judgment on tasks without clear empirical feedback. The researchers also find that making evaluations more realistic tends to make them more expensive to build, run and score. That creates a measurement problem for anyone judging AI readiness: success on a crisp, quickly checked problem does not settle how well an agent will handle ambiguous work. METR reports substantial uncertainty about how these results translate into accelerated AI research and finds no strong overall acceleration or deceleration on its composite public-model capability measure.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Connected devices

A cloud fix cannot clean up every compromised router

GL.iNet disclosed that an attacker exploited its cloud remote-management services to install malicious software on some routers and KVM devices, which provide remote access to connected computers. In its advisory updated November 23, the company says it has blocked that attack path, but already infected devices still need the appropriate security firmware to remove the identified malware. The incident shows how a central management service can become a route into equipment at the network’s edge, and why repairing the service and recovering individual devices are separate jobs. The company is still investigating the scope, including possible effects on computers attached to affected KVMs, and cautions that a clean quick check cannot guarantee a device was never compromised.

STORY 04 · Space · Engineering

An engine that will never fly helps prepare the next Moon missions

A developmental RS-25 engine completed a planned 200-second firing at NASA’s Stennis Space Center on November 18. The engine itself is not destined for spaceflight; its job is to supply performance and reliability data for the engines that will power future Artemis missions. It is a useful reminder that progress toward the Moon also happens in repeatable ground experiments, where engineers can examine hardware before committing it to a crewed launch.

STORY 05 · New York · Community organizations

New York finds $10.8 million that nonprofits may have left behind

New York’s attorney general and comptroller have identified more than 4,000 charities and nonprofits that may be entitled to approximately $10.8 million in unclaimed funds. The offices are contacting organizations about amounts ranging from $20 to $792,000, including groups working in education, health care, housing and the arts. Name changes, mergers and leadership turnover can separate an organization from its money, so recovering these balances offers a practical source of support without creating a new grant program. The announced total is money potentially owed, not a claim that all of it has already been returned.
