STORY 01 · New York · Veterans & careers

Military driving experience gets a clearer road to a civilian license

New York announced on Veterans Day that it is joining the federal Even Exchange program, allowing qualified military applicants to use their training to satisfy the knowledge requirement for a commercial driver license without taking the DMV written permit test. The program will complement an existing military skills-test waiver, which can remove the road-test requirement for eligible applicants with recent experience operating equivalent vehicles. The change addresses a practical transition problem: people who already know how to operate heavy vehicles can face duplicated testing when moving into civilian work, although eligibility requirements still apply.

STORY 02 · AI · Agriculture

An AI search for weed killers will have to prove itself in the greenhouse

An MIT-led team has received an award of up to $53 million from ARPA-H for PLOW, a project to develop alternatives to existing herbicides using generative AI. The planned workflow links molecular design to laboratory screening and greenhouse experiments, with screening results feeding back into the models. Researchers aim to account for weed resistance, manufacturing cost and risks to people and ecosystems together, rather than optimizing only whether a molecule kills a plant. This is funding for a research program, not evidence that a safer commercial weed killer has already been developed.

STORY 03 · Higher education · Materials research

A little structural disorder can make a material better at taking a hit

Researchers at MIT and Northeastern have designed metamaterials around irregular networks of differently sized beams, taking inspiration from patterns such as leaf veins instead of repeating one identical unit. In physical tests reported in a November 11 study, the materials resisted sudden impacts better than conventional ordered metamaterials and cushioned fragile objects embedded inside them. The work points toward protective packaging, helmets and other impact-absorbing structures, but the reported result is a material-design approach rather than a certified protective product.

STORY 04 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

A signed script can still fail the next security check

Microsoft’s November 10 Windows Server 2022 update tightens encoding validation for PowerShell script signing and signature verification. The release notes warn that scripts containing invalid UTF-8 may now fail signature checks even if earlier versions accepted them, and describe saving affected scripts as valid UTF-8 with a byte order mark before signing them again. For enterprise teams, the detail makes automation compatibility part of patch planning: a security improvement can expose assumptions buried in long-running maintenance scripts.

STORY 05 · Science · Earth observation

A reservoir’s rapid recovery carries a flood warning in the same picture

A November 11 NASA Earth Observatory report compares Landsat images showing Navajo Reservoir’s dramatic expansion after heavy autumn rainfall in the Four Corners region. Between September 20 and November 7, its water level rose nearly 60 feet; by November 7, it held 108 percent of the average storage for that date, not 108 percent of its total capacity. The storms eased short-term drought while causing damaging river floods, illustrating why a rising reservoir can be welcome for water supply and dangerous for nearby communities at the same time. Long-term drought persists in parts of the region, so the striking satellite comparison does not establish a lasting end to water scarcity.
