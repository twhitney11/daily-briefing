STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY

Two watersheds become one shared classroom

SUNY announced November 6 that ten University at Buffalo students and ten University of Puerto Rico at Mayagüez students will join a hydrology field exchange in spring 2027. Each group will spend seven days at the other campus, comparing Puerto Rico’s tropical watersheds with Buffalo’s groundwater and aquifers. This is a separate development from yesterday’s conservation-internship announcement: a reciprocal, faculty-supported research exchange that lets students compare water systems they could not study in a single location.

STORY 02 · AI · Materials research

A better battery needs more than the fastest-moving ions

A November 6 Tohoku University announcement highlights a research Perspective proposing an integrated AI workflow for discovering solid electrolytes, the materials that carry ions inside solid-state batteries. The authors connect materials databases, simulations, language models and automated experiments in a feedback loop, while emphasizing stability, mechanical strength and manufacturability alongside ionic conductivity. The paper, published October 13, offers a research framework rather than a demonstrated new battery or a promised performance gain. Its useful shift is toward recording how a material was made and tested, so promising predictions can become reproducible experiments.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise trust

A familiar voice can be part of an unfamiliar fraud

Federal prosecutors announced charges November 5 against three people accused of participating in a scheme that used synthetic voices or images to impersonate executives and lawyers and steal approximately $129 million from companies. According to the indictment, employees were drawn into supposedly confidential transactions and then directed to fake attorneys who instructed them to wire funds to conspirators’ accounts. The case illustrates why a convincing call alone cannot establish that a payment request is legitimate: the apparent second person confirming the transaction can also be an impersonation. These are allegations, and the defendants are presumed innocent unless proven guilty.

STORY 04 · Space · Planetary remnants

A dead star’s double ring hints at worlds that survived

Research highlighted by ScienceDaily on November 6 describes two distinct debris rings around the white dwarf J1328−1450, an unusual structure identified with ESO’s Very Large Telescope. ESO’s October 27 announcement explains that the rings differ chemically and may be maintained by two surviving rocky bodies; those proposed survivors have not been directly observed. The material could come from different parent bodies or different layers of one disrupted object, leaving the history open to investigation. The system offers a possible example of what can remain after a star like the Sun dies, rather than a definite prediction of our own planets’ fate.

STORY 05 · New York · Wildlife surveillance

One deer test triggers a wider search in Livingston County

DEC announced November 6 that chronic wasting disease was detected in a wild deer harvested in Sparta, Livingston County—the first reported wild-deer occurrence in New York since 2005. Cornell’s veterinary laboratory tested and retested the sample, while additional federal laboratory testing was still underway when the announcement was issued. DEC is establishing a six-town containment area and requiring samples from harvested deer there, with on-site collection scheduled to begin November 12. The immediate question is how far the disease extends: hunter-submitted samples will help shape the response, and DEC’s current guidance is the place to check evolving collection and transport requirements.
