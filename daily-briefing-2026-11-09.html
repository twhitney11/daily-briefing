STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY

First-generation students can find their predecessors at the front of the classroom

SUNY marked First-Generation College Day on November 8 by recognizing not only students but also campus presidents, faculty and staff who were themselves the first in their families to attend college. The statement connects that recognition to existing access and support programs, including EOP, ASAP|ACE and SUNY Reconnect, rather than announcing a new grant or eligibility change. Its useful campus-level implication is that first-generation experience need not be invisible once someone becomes an educator or administrator: making those paths visible can give newcomers people to learn from.

STORY 02 · AI · Scientific computing

Sand, water and proteins get a shared simulation language

MIT and collaborating researchers introduced WorldParticle on November 9, a deep-learning system that represents different materials as particles and predicts how they move. The team demonstrated the approach across six physical phenomena, including deformable objects, granular materials and proteins, with a shared architecture rather than a separate hand-built solver for each. That matters when materials interact, such as liquid pushing against a flexible valve, because combining different simulation tools can become a problem of its own. The researchers describe a step toward a general simulation pipeline; broader applications such as galaxy mergers remain ambitions, not demonstrated capabilities in this announcement.

STORY 03 · Digital safety · AI policy

Stopping deepfake abuse means following the services that sustain it

A Brown University study publicized November 9 maps 11 technology categories behind AI-generated nonconsensual intimate imagery, spanning creation, distribution, discovery, infrastructure and payment services. The researchers analyzed 64 state and federal laws and found that more than 92 percent focus on individual creators and distributors, leaving much of the supporting ecosystem comparatively unaddressed. Their argument shifts attention toward the platforms and services that enable abuse, giving policymakers and technology companies more places to consider prevention before images spread. This is a research framework and call for further interventions, not evidence that a new technical safeguard or legal remedy has already solved the problem.

STORY 04 · Space · Mission design

Two Mars probes turn a year of waiting into a powered slingshot

NASA confirmed November 9 that both ESCAPADE spacecraft completed powered Earth flybys, combining gravity assists with engine burns to begin their journey toward Mars. After launching in November 2025, the pair spent time in a looping Earth-proximity orbit before returning for maneuvers on November 7 and 9, demonstrating a route that can loosen the dependence on conventional direct-to-Mars launch windows. The spacecraft are targeting Mars arrival in September 2027, with science operations expected to start in June 2028 to examine how the solar wind drives atmospheric loss. The flybys are accomplished milestones; arrival and coordinated observations from two locations around Mars are still ahead.

STORY 05 · New York · Conservation

Protecting land also means funding the people who look after it

New York DEC announced $2.7 million in competitive Conservation Partnership Program funding on November 6 for qualified nonprofit land-conservation organizations. The five grant categories include permanent staffing, organizational capacity, stewardship and public-access improvements, partnerships, and indirect costs associated with land or conservation-easement acquisitions. That breadth matters because conserving a property creates continuing work: maintaining access, caring for habitat and keeping the responsible organization capable of doing both. The announcement opens a funding opportunity administered by the Land Trust Alliance with DEC; it does not identify a new round of completed awards or protected parcels.
