STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY

A bicycle becomes a rolling STEM laboratory

SUNY Geneseo announced a $96,000 National Science Foundation grant for Jennifer Tripp’s part in STEMCyclists, a collaboration with University at Buffalo researchers and community cycling groups. Nearly 100 Buffalo-area teens are expected to participate over three years, building and customizing bicycles while exploring physics, biomechanics and engineering through a summer program and follow-up rides. The research opportunity is as interesting as the workshop: the team will study what young people learn when scientific ideas become machines they can change and ride.

STORY 02 · Artificial intelligence · Accountability

Bad chatbot advice can disappear from the medical record

A perspective paper highlighted by Binghamton University argues that harmful AI health advice can remain hidden from the clinicians and researchers who might otherwise investigate it. The authors call for users to be able to retrieve and share their conversations, alongside systems for reporting and investigating problematic guidance. This is a proposal for better oversight, not a measurement of how often chatbots harm patients; its central insight is that evaluating an answer is only part of safety if nobody can reconstruct what happened afterward.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

PHP’s emergency fixes reach below the application layer

PHP released versions 8.5.12, 8.4.27, 8.3.36 and 8.2.35 on October 21 to fix two critical vulnerabilities that can permit unauthenticated remote code execution. The PHP Foundation says an exploit against Apache with mod_php can strike before application code runs, illustrating why keeping a website’s plugins current does not necessarily protect the runtime underneath them. The project urges immediate updates and says partial mitigations do not make installations safe; administrators should check their operating-system or hosting vendor’s patched packages as well as the PHP version.

STORY 04 · Science · Space weather

A 16-minute train delay exposes a seven-year error in space-weather history

Research revisited in an October 21 ScienceDaily report traces a famous Exeter train delay to a likely date of October 18, 1848, rather than 1841: the railway in the original account had not yet opened in 1841. The study, published in September, combines timetables, newspapers and magnetic observations to explain how a solar disturbance could have disrupted telegraph signals and held the train for 16 minutes. The correction removes the incident’s claim to be the earliest recorded technological space-weather disruption, while showing why old archives still matter to understanding a hazard faced by modern electrical infrastructure.

STORY 05 · New York · Cannabis industry

New York’s cannabis payment records face a quiet cutoff tomorrow

New York’s Office of Cannabis Management says October 22 is the deadline to report delinquent payments for credit transfers made before September 15 through its older COD Reporting platform. Its October 1 board presentation says that delinquency-reporting function will close October 23, while reports of full payment for those older transfers will remain available; transfers or deliveries from September 15 onward use Metrc. The distinction matters because a growing regulated market depends on reliable supplier-payment records: the cutoff concerns where and when older delinquencies can be reported, rather than announcing that those debts disappear.
