STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY

Law students practice the work of repairing harm

At the University at Buffalo, ten second- and third-year law students are studying restorative justice through a course that starts with discussion circles and invites them to help shape the agenda. Professor Kim Diana Connolly pairs practice in listening and accountability with research projects and simulated interviews with real reporters, asking students to explain their ideas beyond the classroom. The November 13 campus report offers a concrete look at legal education focused on what happens after harm: how people describe its impact, accept responsibility and consider repair. The course illustrates an approach to professional training; the report does not establish that it produces better case outcomes.

STORY 02 · AI · Scientific tools

A chemistry dataset puts specialized AI to a more specific test

NVIDIA announced Nemotron for Science today, combining its Nemotron 3.5 Super VL model with scientific evaluations, a new chemistry dataset and instructions for adapting models to specialized research. The company says LitmusBench v0.2 contains nearly 50,000 questions for both training and evaluating chemistry reasoning, drawing on molecular properties and experimental drug-discovery data. The interesting shift is toward tools that researchers can adapt and assess for a particular job, such as reasoning about molecular behavior, rather than relying on a general chatbot’s fluency. These are the supplier’s announced resources and use cases, not independent proof that an agent can reliably conduct scientific research.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

The patch itself is becoming an attacker’s research material

Google’s threat researchers report finding an exposed server running an AI-assisted framework that compares web-application code before and after patches, identifies possible flaws, drafts exploits and executes them automatically. The team says victim data on the server suggested an interest in French media, entertainment and education websites, making this an observed operation rather than just a laboratory demonstration. The broader implication for enterprise IT is a shorter interval between a publicly explained fix and attempts to exploit organizations that have not installed it. Google also describes a flood of nonworking exploit claims around other vulnerabilities, a reminder that automated output still needs verification.

STORY 04 · Space · Open research data

A billion-object survey gives astronomers a new starting point

ESA has released Euclid’s DR1-Foundation dataset, drawing on about 13 months of observations and including more than 900 million galaxies and around 15,000 strong gravitational-lensing candidates. Those distorted views of distant objects can help researchers investigate the mass of foreground galaxies, including dark matter that cannot be seen directly. The release covers about 14 percent of the area planned for the mission’s survey, giving astronomers a large shared collection to search for rare objects and begin constructing a three-dimensional cosmic map. ESA expects the fuller release in mid-2027 to add weak-lensing observations and further information about how galaxies fit into the cosmic web.

STORY 05 · New York · Water and public policy

A creek’s new category would raise the bar—but not to swimming

New York’s proposed reclassification of Newtown Creek reaches a scheduled public hearing today, November 16, with written comments open through November 30. The change from Class SD to Class I would add water-quality protections for boating and fish propagation, according to the state’s regulatory impact statement. The same document makes a crucial distinction: Class I does not include standards protective of swimming, so a stronger designation would not amount to a declaration that the creek is safe for swimmers. The proposal remains under review, illustrating how a technical category sets the environmental goals that permits and cleanup planning must consider.
