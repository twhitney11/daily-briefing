STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY

A campus pantry is only useful when students can reach it

SUNY is extending refrigerated food lockers to five more campuses, giving students a way to collect pantry food outside normal opening hours. The expansion builds on a program that already includes Plattsburgh and Potsdam, while a separate effort with Trellis Strategies will help campuses address transportation gaps. These are practical barriers to completing a degree: an evening student can miss both the pantry’s hours and the last useful bus, even when support exists on paper.

STORY 02 · AI · Research

The interesting AI drug-discovery result may be a better experiment

Anthropic described six drug-development partnerships that use Claude across molecular design, laboratory analysis and the paperwork surrounding research. In one BigHat Biosciences project, scientists and an Anthropic researcher used Claude to revisit questionable measurement scores, propose revised metrics and design a laboratory check, rather than simply optimize molecules against the original scores. The company’s account suggests that AI’s value could include improving how a question is tested, but these are early, mostly preclinical case studies and do not establish safe or effective new medicines for patients.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Enterprise IT

Cloud security checks are catching up with the AI inside office software

CISA announced updates to ScubaGear and ScubaGoggles, its tools for assessing Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace settings against security baselines. The Google Workspace work expands Gemini coverage, while the project’s release notes also document checks for Assured Controls and fixes to existing assessments. For administrators, the significance is that an office suite’s security posture now includes its embedded AI features; repeatable configuration checks can expose gaps that a one-time deployment review misses.

STORY 04 · Science · Climate

The national warming average hides very different state-level changes

An MIT-led study analyzed climate and weather datasets covering 1980–2022 to estimate monthly temperature trends for every U.S. state. Alaska, Utah, New Mexico, Colorado and Nevada showed the strongest average warming trends, with seasonal differences that a single annual national number would obscure. The dataset is intended to help planners identify where heat-sensitive infrastructure, ecosystems and other resources may approach their limits sooner; it describes historical patterns rather than predicting the weather for a particular day.

STORY 05 · New York · Outdoors

An autumn campfire can survive underground long after it looks out

New York’s DEC warned that unusually dry conditions have elevated fire danger, after rangers encountered wildfires linked to unattended or incompletely extinguished campfires. In dry ground, embers can smolder for days or weeks before an obvious wildfire appears, which makes the absence of visible flames a poor test of safety. The agency urges people to use established fire rings if they have a fire, keep it small, and thoroughly soak and stir the remains until cool; the warning is especially relevant as hunters and leaf-peepers head into the woods.
