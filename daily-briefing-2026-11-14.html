STORY 01 · Higher education · SUNY / North Country

A paid history fellowship leaves something useful in the local museum

SUNY Potsdam’s new account of its summer history fellowships shows five students contributing to museums and historians across Northern and Central New York, as part of a 60-student program spanning three SUNY campuses. Projects included a digital inventory across four museums in Clay, research on Black labor in Clinton County, and exhibit interpretation in St. Lawrence and Lewis counties. With students receiving $4,800 for 150 hours of work, the program connects paid academic experience to the often painstaking work that makes local collections accessible to the public.

STORY 02 · AI · Robotics

The useful warehouse robot may be the one you can steer by hand

MIT’s new profile of Robust AI describes Carter, a cart-like robot already working in warehouses and factories alongside people. Its vision system, force-sensitive handlebar and wheels that move in any direction let workers guide it physically, while autonomous transport can take over longer trips between stations. The interesting design choice is to fit automation around existing workers and spaces, making human intervention part of normal operation rather than treating it as a failure.

STORY 03 · Cybersecurity · Embedded computing

A smaller computer gets security designed into its foundations

MIT Lincoln Laboratory’s newly announced R&D 100 winners include Magnetite, an operating system for embedded computers such as those in satellites and flight systems. It combines a formally verified seL4 microkernel with services written in Rust and isolated processes that receive only the access they need. The aim is to contain a compromised component while critical functions continue, a consequential distinction when a computer controls physical equipment. The laboratory says integration into space and flight platforms is underway; the mathematical verification applies to the microkernel, not a promise that every complete system is invulnerable.

STORY 04 · Space · Public science

Endeavour’s new mission starts with visitors looking up

The California Science Center says its Samuel Oschin Air and Space Center opened to the public on November 13, with Endeavour displayed upright alongside authentic boosters and an external tank. The 200,000-square-foot expansion pairs aerospace artifacts with 100 hands-on exhibits, including an opportunity to program a Mars rover. The opening turns a retired flight vehicle into a setting for exploring how spaceflight works, connecting the scale of the hardware with experiments visitors can try themselves.

STORY 05 · Enterprise IT · Public services

An email outage can outlast the return of the inbox

Maine’s Judicial Branch says its email outage ran from about 1 p.m. November 12 until about 7 a.m. November 13, as part of the wider Microsoft Outlook disruption. Its notice warns that emailed protection-order requests submitted after 12:30 p.m. on November 12 may not have arrived, and asks affected filers to consider resubmitting by email or on paper. The notice illustrates why recovery is more than restoring a service: organizations also need a way to find and reconcile urgent work that went missing while it was unavailable.
